Tier 3 — targeted / therapeutic
Tier 3 — Desiccated Beef Liver
Natural vitamin A · replaces EVCLO · Amazon UK · ~£20–30/month

EVCLO (fermented cod liver oil) is primarily valued for its natural preformed vitamin A (retinol) and fat-soluble vitamin cofactors — not its omega-3 content, which is too low to be therapeutic. PROM3GA covers the omega-3. The missing piece is vitamin A. Desiccated grass-fed beef liver is the most natural, whole-food way to replace it — liver is the densest retinol source in existence, far richer than cod liver oil per gram.

Milk thistle note: Vitamin A is processed by the liver. At normal supplemental doses from food-based sources this is entirely safe, and your milk thistle provides an additional hepatoprotective layer — a reassuring combination. Don't exceed 4 caps/day (standard serving) to keep retinol within a safe range.
Buy: Look for grass-fed, freeze-dried on Amazon UK — brands like Wilderland, Ancestral Supplements, or similar. Freeze-dried preserves the nutrient profile without heat damage. Avoid anything with fillers or "desiccated liver powder" in a proprietary blend with no dose transparency. ~£20–30/month for 4 caps/day. Alternatively: eat 100g lamb or beef liver once a week — far cheaper, higher retinol, and more bioavailable than any capsule.
Tier 3 — Milk Thistle
Liver support · ALT elevation · bulk.com · ~£4–5/month · already taking

Your ALT of 33 is above the functional range of 0–26 — a mild liver enzyme elevation, almost certainly why milk thistle was recommended. Silymarin (the active compound) is well-evidenced for reducing elevated liver enzymes and protecting hepatocytes from oxidative stress. Already in your stack — just documenting it here for completeness and sourcing.

Milk thistle improves bile flow, which increases absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (your D3+K2, vitamin A from liver capsules). No negative interactions with omega-3, methylation complex, magnesium, zinc, or any other supplement in this plan.
Buy: Milk Thistle Extract Tablets — bulk.com · ~£4–5/month. Standardised to 80% silymarin — the form used in clinical trials. If you're already buying bulk.com orders, add this in; it's negligible cost.
Tier 3 — Curcumin + Piperine
CRP / systemic inflammation · bulk.com · ~£5–6/month

Your CRP-hs of 6.9 is more than double the lab upper limit of 3.0. Curcumin (the active compound in turmeric) has the strongest RCT evidence of any supplement for reducing CRP — multiple trials show meaningful reductions at 500–1,000mg/day. It works by inhibiting NF-κB, a key inflammatory signalling pathway.

Critical: piperine is not optional. Plain curcumin has near-zero bioavailability — it passes through almost unabsorbed. Piperine (black pepper extract) increases absorption by ~2,000%. Any curcumin product without piperine (or a specialist delivery system like BCM-95/Meriva) is a waste of money. bulk.com's product combines both.
Buy: Curcumin & Piperine — bulk.com · ~£5–6/month · take with food (fat increases absorption further). This is the cheapest addition to your plan and directly targets the most elevated marker in your blood work.
Tier 3 — Methylation Complex
High homocysteine · MTHFR · active forms only
Context: MTHFR mutation and high homocysteine. MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) is a very common genetic variant that reduces your ability to convert folate into its active form (5-MTHF). This impairs the methylation cycle, which is one of the main routes the body uses to clear homocysteine. Supplementing with active, pre-methylated forms bypasses the genetic block entirely. The critical point: folic acid and cyanocobalamin (the forms in every mainstream supplement) are useless here — you need 5-MTHF and methylcobalamin, plus ideally riboflavin (B2 as R5P, a required MTHFR cofactor) and TMG/betaine as a backup methylation pathway.
None of bulk.com, Myprotein, or Holland & Barrett stock the active forms. This is a separate purchase from a specialist retailer.

If MTHFR is confirmed (or strongly suspected): Thorne Methyl-Guard Plus is the standout — 1,000µg methylfolate (2.5× the others), 1,000µg methylcobalamin, active B2 as a cofactor for the MTHFR enzyme itself, and 3,000mg TMG which opens up an entirely separate methylation bypass route. It's the highest-rated product for this specific use case. The cost is high at £79–92/month but it's doing substantially more work than the alternatives.

If you want to stay on Metagenics: It's a genuinely good product — practitioner-grade, correct active forms, solid brand. At ~£21/month it's 4× cheaper than Thorne. The gaps vs Thorne are: lower doses of both methylfolate and B12, no riboflavin-5-phosphate, and a lower TMG amount. For moderate homocysteine elevation it may be entirely sufficient; for a confirmed MTHFR homozygous variant the higher doses in Thorne may give a meaningfully better response.

Cytoplan at ~£13/month is worth knowing about — same doses as Metagenics but adds active B2 (which Metagenics lacks), and it's cheaper. If you're looking for a step up from Metagenics without going to Thorne prices, Cytoplan is the middle ground.

Riboflavin (B2) — is it covered? Riboflavin-5-phosphate (R5P) is a required cofactor for the MTHFR enzyme — without it, the enzyme works less efficiently even when you're supplying plenty of methylfolate. Metagenics does not include B2 — if you stay on it, riboflavin is an uncovered gap. Cytoplan Methyl Factors does include R5P — switching to Cytoplan covers riboflavin, costs less, and removes the need to add a separate B2 supplement. If you stay on Metagenics, consider adding a standalone riboflavin-5-phosphate supplement (~£5–8/month on Amazon UK).
Why bulk.com, Myprotein, and H&B won't work here: All three use folic acid (not methylfolate) and cyanocobalamin (not methylcobalamin) — the inactive forms. If your MTHFR enzyme is impaired, you can't convert folic acid to 5-MTHF anyway. This isn't a quality-vs-price trade-off — the inactive forms simply don't address the underlying issue regardless of dose.
