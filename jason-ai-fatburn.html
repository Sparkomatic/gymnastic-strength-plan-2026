Ten moves. Two swaps. Two rooms.

Every session in this programme is the same session. The same warm-up, the same six blocks, the same ten moves in the same order. Only two moves change from workout to workout — one explosive, one strength. That's the whole design.

It means you can hand this to anyone. There is no vocabulary to learn, no page to keep reading mid-workout, and by week two you'll be running it from memory. Which matters more than it sounds: the time you spend reading a page is time you're not working, and confident technique is what lets you actually push hard.

1 · Fat Break — release it
Short, hard, whole-body bursts. Hard efforts flood the system with adrenaline and noradrenaline, and those hormones switch on the enzymes that break stored fat down and push it into the bloodstream. You can't burn fat you haven't released — so this comes early, and again later.
2 · Fat Burn — keep the muscle
Controlled resistance work at moderate effort. In a deficit the body drops whatever it isn't using. Loading the muscle through full range, five times a week, is the signal that says keep this. It decides whether you finish lean or just smaller.
3 · Armour — stay in the game
Four mobility positions, identical every session, slotted where you'd otherwise stand around recovering. Costs no time, adds no fatigue. Repeating the same four positions is precisely what changes your range — mobility rewards consistency, not variety.
4 · Oxidise — burn it off
Steady, conversational work at the end, not the start. Fatty acids are already circulating by now, and moderate intensity is where the body burns the highest proportion of fat for fuel. Doing this first would waste the whole effect.

The order is the point. Hard bursts release the fat, the steady finisher burns it, the strength work between protects the muscle, the mobility keeps you healthy enough to repeat it. Train fasted — insulin is low first thing, and low insulin is when fat releases most freely.

Workouts 1 & 2 — lounge, 5am
Built to run in a room in a sleeping house. No jumping, no landings, no bell hitting the floorboards. The explosive work is a step-up onto the stack and floor-based climbers — both genuinely hard, neither audible. Pulling is the inverted row under a table.
Workouts 3 & 4 — gym
Same six blocks, same ten moves, two things the lounge cannot give you: real jumping (burpees, skater hops) on a floor that can take it, and a pull-up bar — the vertical pull replaces the table row in Block 2. That one change closes the only real gap in the programme.

The split costs you nothing. The lounge sessions are not a watered-down version: a hard explosive step-up and a heavy swing move the same hormones a squat jump does, and they are kinder to the knees you are asking to do pistol squats. The gym sessions are strictly better than the original, because they add the vertical pulling it was missing. Either room, it is the same session — you still only learn it once.

Equipment, lounge: one kettlebell, the floor, a sturdy table (or anything with a hip-height edge) and your adjustable stack. Nothing else. The stack does three jobs — it is the platform for the explosive step-up, the height adjuster that scales your push-ups, and the way you load the inverted row harder by putting your feet up on it. No rings needed anywhere; if you have them, the row swap is noted.

Equipment, gym: all of the above plus a pull-up bar, which is the only thing the gym is genuinely needed for. Impact is kept moderate throughout: capped jump volume, soft landings, no repeated hard impacts — and no landings at all in the lounge sessions.

Three lounge sessions
Just alternate the pair: WO 1 → WO 2 → WO 1, then start the next week on WO 2.
Two gym sessions
WO 3 and WO 4, on whichever two days you have access. Order does not matter.

Fit the workout to the room, not to a fixed cycle. Five sessions a week: the two days you can get to a gym are WO 3 and WO 4, the other three are the lounge pair alternating. A typical week runs 1 · 3 · 2 · 4 · 1. Because the sessions are near-identical it genuinely doesn't matter if you lose track — the only rule worth keeping is don't run the same workout two days running. If a gym day falls through, do a lounge session and take the pull-ups next time.

How to progress: keep the structure fixed and change one thing at a time. Weeks 1–2, learn the moves and hold the numbers. Then, in this order — add reps to the Fat Burn block, then add load, then cut the recovery between Fat Break rounds. Don't cut recovery first: the hard blocks only work if they're genuinely hard, and they can't be if you never recover.

Short on time? Drop Block 3 to a single round. That's your 40-minute version and it costs you least. Want the bigger burn? Take the Oxidise block from 8 minutes to 16–18 — the session runs ~55 minutes and matches BellyProof's total output.

What this version gains
Near-zero learning burden. Technique improves fast through repetition, and good technique means you can safely push harder and load heavier — which tends to raise real-world intensity, not lower it. Progress is trackable because the numbers are directly comparable week to week.
What it gives up
Less exercise variety means the same tissues take load more often — the two rotating slots and the fixed mobility block exist specifically to offset that, but it's the real cost. Vertical pulling only happens on the two gym days, so a week where you never reach a gym gives you none. There is also less breadth of trunk and shoulder work than a longer, more varied programme.

An independent workout built on the same underlying principles as BellyProof — not the BellyProof programme. General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Heavy single-leg work and maximal pulling are deliberately left out so these sessions don't eat into pistol squat and ring muscle-up training — the gym-day pull-ups are capped at 5–6 comfortable reps for exactly that reason. If your main plan has a heavy pull that day, do the table row here instead.

SWAP
What Changes
2 moves per session
Eight rotating moves, two per workout The only difference between the four sessions
Two slots rotate: one explosive move in the Fat Break blocks, one strength move in Fat Burn. They exist to stop the same tissues taking the same load five days a week, and to cover what the core ten cannot — overhead pressing, rotation and shoulder stability. The Lounge pair are silent and landing-free; the Gym pair use the floor and the bar.
